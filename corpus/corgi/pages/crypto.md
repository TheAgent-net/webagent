## Crypto Claims ScenariosWallet Failures, Security Compromises, and Funds-Transfer Fraud

### The wallet failure

A wallet workflow malfunctions: transactions fail, balances misreport, or withdrawals stall, and customers allege platform negligence. This is the Technology E&O conversation.

### The wallet compromise

An attacker gains unauthorized access to keys or signing infrastructure and assets are stolen. This is a Cyber and Crime/Fidelity conversation, not an E&O one.

### The bridge failure

A smart-contract or validator issue disrupts transfers and users seek recovery for financial loss.

### The funds-transfer fraud

A social-engineering attack compromises approvals and triggers a Crime/Fidelity and Cyber review.

## FAQ

What insurance does a crypto or Web3 startup need?keyboard\_arrow\_down

Crypto and Web3 startups typically evaluate [Technology Errors & Omissions](https://www.corgi.insure/tech-eo) for software and platform failures, Cyber Liability for security and data incidents, [Crime / Fidelity](https://www.corgi.insure/crime) for funds-transfer and employee-theft exposures, [D&O](https://www.corgi.insure/directors-and-officers) for leadership and investor claims, and General Liability for ordinary business operations. Coverage depends on underwriting, business model, custody, controls, and policy terms.

Does Corgi support crypto and Web3 companies?keyboard\_arrow\_down

Yes. Corgi underwrites crypto and Web3 companies as specialized technology risks, with dedicated review of custody architecture, smart-contract exposure, and financial-crime controls - not a generic software quote.

What is Technology E&O for crypto companies?keyboard\_arrow\_down

[Technology E&O](https://www.corgi.insure/tech-eo) can respond to customer allegations that a negligent act, error, or omission in your wallet software, protocol tooling, API, or Web3 product caused them financial harm. Digital-asset operations such as custody, exchange, and transfer typically require an affirmative digital-asset endorsement, and smart-contract, custody, and token-related claims require careful underwriting and policy wording.

Do crypto companies need Crime / Fidelity coverage?keyboard\_arrow\_down

Often, yes. Companies that handle money movement, payment flows, private keys, admin access, or digital-asset operations should evaluate [Crime / Fidelity](https://www.corgi.insure/crime) alongside Cyber. Cyber usually focuses on systems and data, while Crime / Fidelity is where funds-transfer fraud, social engineering, and employee dishonesty are evaluated.

Is digital asset custody automatically covered?keyboard\_arrow\_down

No. Digital asset custody is one of the places where policy wording matters most. Hot wallets, warm wallets, cold storage, MPC signing, key ceremonies, withdrawal governance, and third-party custodians all affect underwriting. Some exposures may require endorsements, sublimits, brokered markets, or manual review.

What cyber insurance do Web3 companies need?keyboard\_arrow\_down

Web3 companies should evaluate [Cyber Liability](https://www.corgi.insure/cyber-liability) for incident response, forensic investigation, business interruption, privacy claims, regulatory defense where available, and security events involving cloud infrastructure, admin panels, wallets, keys, and customer data.

How are smart-contract failures and exploits insured?keyboard\_arrow\_down

A coding error that causes a smart contract to behave incorrectly may raise a Technology E&O claim. An attacker exploiting that error is a Cyber and Crime/Fidelity conversation. Coverage depends on the allegations, custody, controls, endorsements, and policy terms.

When should a crypto startup get coverage?keyboard\_arrow\_down

Start before custody, mainnet launch, exchange listing, institutional onboarding, or a priced funding round. Those milestones tend to trigger heavier diligence around wallet controls, security audits, regulatory posture, leadership liability, and proof of insurance.
